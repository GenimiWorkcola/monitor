【Title】DeepSeek engineer defends push for open-source AGI despite higher risks
【时间】Wed, 04 Nov 2026
----------------------------------------
【中文正文】
Some AI pioneers have warned that technology smarter than humans may escape human control.
DeepSeek engineer Gao Xi speaks on an AI panel at the World Internet Conference on Tuesday. Photo: kankanews
DeepSeek is committed to open-source artificial intelligence to accelerate the arrival of artificial general intelligence (AGI), even if it heightens safety risks, a company engineer says, defying growing safety concerns from the US and scrutiny in China over whether the strategy poses national security risks.
Speaking on Tuesday on an AI panel at the World Internet Conference (WIC) in Wuzhen, DeepSeek engineer Gao Xi said open-source development promoted shared innovations and technical breakthroughs, framing the technology’s rapid progress as a race against the clock, rather than against rival firms.
“If AGI is going to arrive in 10 years, then I hope we can bring this forward to one year. If it is going to arrive in one year, then I hope it can arrive tomorrow,” Gao said, in rare remarks from a DeepSeek representative.
“AI is developing faster every year. For AI model companies, the real competitor in the AI race is not other companies, but time,” Gao said. He added that technical barriers erected by competitors would only temporarily slow competition, not stop the inevitable arrival of AGI.
Gao’s remarks come as China’s open-source push emerges as a key flashpoint in the US-China tech rivalry. Unlike closed-weight systems championed by American front-runners such as OpenAI and Anthropic, open-weight models allow anyone with sufficient hardware to download and modify the code without vendor oversight.
The opening ceremony of the 2026 World Internet Conference in Wuzhen, in China’s eastern Zhejiang province. Photo: Xinhua
While Beijing has supported open-source ecosystems as a viable way to counter US tech containment, security experts have cautioned that releasing model weights could lower the barrier for rogue actors to conduct cyberattacks or develop bioweapons.
At the same time, some AI pioneers – including Turing Award winners Geoffrey Hinton and Yoshua Bengio – have warned that AGI may escape human control.
Acknowledging that open-source releases “may amplify the impact of safety issues”, Gao said DeepSeek was working to strengthen safety features, including classifications for its models, though he did not provide specific details.
The “irreversible” nature of open-weight models presented additional safety challenges, acknowledged Liu Dayiheng, project lead for Alibaba Group Holding’s open-weight Qwen models, who also appeared on the panel.
However, Liu pushed back against the closed ecosystem model favoured in the West, arguing that the solution lay in a comprehensive governance regime and industry standards rather than technological gatekeeping.
“Without open source, the core technologies of large language models would only be in the hands of a few companies,” Liu said. “Open source allows developers around the world to contribute technology and innovate together.”
Alibaba owns the South China Morning Post.
[embedded media]
Beijing is balancing both sides. While authorities proposed new export controls last month targeting frontier AI models with advanced cyber-offensive capabilities, top officials maintained support for open-source software.
Both DeepSeek and Shanghai-based MiniMax completed domestic registrations to open-source their latest models, state media reported last month.
This year’s WIC, convened under the theme “Open Source and Openness”, drew around 1,800 delegates from 138 countries and regions, according to official figures.
Addressing the opening ceremony on Tuesday, Premier Li Qiang said China would continue to pursue “open innovation, promote technology transfer ... and work with all to build an open, inclusive and dynamic global ecosystem for digital innovation”.
The AI panel was moderated by Wang Jian, an academician at the Chinese Academy of Engineering and chairman of the WIC’s AI committee, as well as founder of Alibaba Cloud. Other panellists included Wen Feng, chief architect and head of open platform at MiniMax, and John Higgins, chairman of the International AI Governance Association.
At a separate WIC forum on Wednesday, Unitree Robotics founder Wang Xingxing said much of the data collected by robots remained underused, adding that self-improving physical AI models could help make better use of the data to accelerate technological advances.

It was not ‌immediately clear how close the telescope’s re-entry point was to North Sumatra’s capital and largest city, Medan, home to about 2.5 million people. The population of the province as a whole totals around 16 million.

Nasa’s prized US$500 million Swift satellite was primarily designed to study ⁠gamma-ray bursts – the most intense blasts of energy in the universe – using its multi-wavelength array of three telescopes.

For Finland and Norway, direct contact with Russia is not new, although the two countries have different experiences of managing the relationship. Finland, which joined Nato in 2023, has taken a firm position towards Russia while leaving open the possibility of future engagement. Finland shares a long land border, making changes in Russian military deployments and regional security arrangements especially relevant to its defence planning. Norway faces different challenges in the High North, where military activity, maritime security and cross-border relations remain important.
A peace settlement in Ukraine might end the fighting, but difficult questions about Russia’s military presence and Nato’s security arrangements in northern Europe will remain. Finland and Norway will have to deal with these issues long after negotiations end.

Finland and Norway emphasised that they had consulted Ukraine before the call and were speaking on behalf of their own governments, not Europe as a whole. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky subsequently welcomed their diplomatic support and discussed preparations for further negotiations.

European countries will have to live with the security consequences of any agreement, even if they are not directly involved in determining its terms. For Russia’s neighbours, direct diplomatic contact provides an additional opportunity to express concerns that might not receive equal attention in broader negotiations.

Countries bordering Russia might have particular reasons to maintain direct contacts, but such initiatives require consultation with relevant partners and clarity about whose interests they represent.
The October 27 call produced no publicly announced breakthrough, but it nevertheless shows how Russia’s neighbours can seek a direct role in diplomacy as negotiations over Ukraine evolve. Finland and Norway will have to manage the security consequences of any settlement long after the talks themselves have ended.
Europe’s task is to make room for those concerns while ensuring national initiatives support a coherent diplomatic effort. Unity over Russia does not require identical channels of communication.

“From Pyongyang’s perspective, even an agreement reached with Trump could be at risk of collapsing at any time. North Korea would therefore seek to avoid a repeat of the 2019 Hanoi summit’s failure,” he said, referring to the collapse of talks after the two sides failed to agree on sanctions relief in exchange for nuclear dismantlement.
US President Donald Trump and North Korean leader Kim Jong-un shake hands during their summit at the Metropole Hotel in Hanoi, Vietnam, in 2019. Photo: Reuters
North Korea had a deep-seated conviction that “the essence of the US hostile policy towards North Korea will never change”, Lim said. Given this distrust, Pyongyang saw little incentive to engage in dialogue with Washington, risking the uncertain implementation of an agreement for limited tangible benefits, he added.
“North Korea is likely to further consolidate a survival structure capable of neutralising US sanctions pressure through comprehensive military and economic cooperation with China and Russia,” Lim said.
Such ties would allow Pyongyang to pursue its defence and economic development plans with less concern for Washington’s reaction while preparing for “a prolonged strategic confrontation”, he added.
“The current political situation is likely to reinforce North Korea’s conviction that developing its independent nuclear capabilities, regardless of changes in the US administration or the ruling party, was the correct and strategically superior choice.”
“The development, test-firing and training of new weapons systems are expected to become increasingly routine and regularised,” Lim said.
Against this backdrop, North Korea’s Wednesday tests paired technical verification of a new air-defence missile system with simulated target strikes by long-range strategic cruise missiles.
Lim said the simultaneous deployment of defensive and offensive systems in a single day was intended to show improved electronic warfare capabilities and technologies for evading low- and high-altitude air-defence networks.
Yang Moo-jin, a professor at the University of North Korean Studies, said it was rare for North Korea to launch defensive surface-to-air missiles together with offensive cruise missiles.
“The near-simultaneous timing of the missile launches and Kim Yo-jong’s statement is also unusual, suggesting that the actions were planned in advance to coincide with the US midterm elections,” Yang said, adding that the launches were intended to draw attention to North Korea and underscore its strategic significance.
The tests also served as a retaliatory show of force against Freedom Flag, a two-week joint South Korea-US air drill that will last until Friday, Yang said.
An F-16 fighter jet lands at Osan Air Base in Pyeongtaek, South Korea, in October, during the joint US-South Korea Freedom Flag air exercise. Photo: EPA
By pairing weapons demonstrations with Kim Yo-jong’s statement, Pyongyang was signalling that its words were backed by action and that it would match any perceived US hostility with corresponding measures, he said.
“Pyongyang is conveying that dialogue and confrontation, peace and military exercises and improving relations and disrespect for its sovereignty cannot coexist,” Yang said.
“However, North Korea is also signalling that dialogue, peace and improved relations remain possible if the Trump administration respects the North Korean regime and abandons what Pyongyang regards as hostile policies towards the country, including joint US-South Korean military exercises.”

【Title】‘China-squeeze narrative’ on trade surplus belies reality, top official says
【时间】Thu, 05 Nov 2026
----------------------------------------
【中文正文】
‘The surplus is in China, but the benefits are shared by all parties,’ vice-premier says while pledging import expansion at opening of CIIE.
China’s Vice-Premier Ding Xuexiang speaks during the opening ceremony of the 9th China International Import Expo in Shanghai on Thursday. Photo: AFP
Chinese Vice-Premier Ding Xuexiang pushed back against criticism of the country’s widening trade surplus while pledging to expand imports, as China faces growing international pressure to address global trade imbalances.
Addressing the opening ceremony of the China International Import Expo (CIIE) in Shanghai on Thursday, Ding said China had never deliberately sought a trade surplus, which he attributed to the country’s position in global supply chains and its comparative advantages.
“The surplus is in China, but the benefits are shared by all parties,” Ding said, adding that foreign companies accounted for 27 per cent of China’s total exports and 16 per cent of its trade surplus in 2025.
“The so-called China-squeeze narrative promoted by some institutions and media outlets is rooted in a zero-sum mentality and is fundamentally untenable.”
In the first nine months of the year, China recorded a trade surplus of US$938.1 billion, customs data showed, putting the country on track to post another record annual surplus after last year’s US$1.2 trillion figure drew heightened scrutiny from Western trading partners.
US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has called for coordinated action among Group of 20 members to put pressure on Beijing over the surplus, raising the prospect of greater external pressure on China at the G20 summit in Miami in December.
The six-day CIIE, launched in 2018, is seen by many foreign businesses as a prime marketing opportunity, as well as a platform for China to signal its commitment to boosting imports and keeping its market open to foreign businesses.
Normal trade in certain hi-tech products has been disrupted and restricted, causing huge losses for all partiesVice-Premier Ding Xuexiang
During his address, Ding called for the removal of trade barriers, warning that “overstretching the concept of national security” would only exacerbate trade imbalances.
“There are many products that China wants to import but cannot buy, or is not allowed to buy,” he said, without citing specific countries. “In particular, normal trade in certain hi-tech products has been disrupted and restricted, causing huge losses for all parties concerned.”
This year’s expo attracted 4,142 companies from around the world and covers more than 440,000 square metres at the National Exhibition and Convention Centre, Vice-Minister of Commerce Yan Dong said at a press conference last month.
More than 300 Fortune Global 500 companies and industry-leading businesses were taking part, a record for the event, while more than 2,000 companies were making their debuts at this year’s event, Yan said.
The National Exhibition and Convention Center in Shanghai, the venue for the China International Import Expo, pictured on Wednesday. Photo: Xinhua
Among exhibitors, companies from the United States, Germany, Britain and France account for the largest exhibition areas. US companies occupy more than 50,000 square metres, retaining the top spot for the eighth consecutive year.
The Hongqiao International Economic Forum, featuring a main forum and 23 sub-forums, is also part of the CIIE’s schedule of events.
Ding said the forum would for the first time release a report on countries’ export opportunities in the Chinese market, providing businesses with a reference for assessing global openness and exploring potential trade.
“China is now both the world’s factory and the world’s market,” he said, pledging that the country would import more high-quality goods and services to “provide impetus and stability for global economic development”.
